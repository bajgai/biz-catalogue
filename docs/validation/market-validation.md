# Market Validation Approach

Public documentation of the method used to validate the Biz Catalogue
product concept. This page describes process only; validation results,
pricing decisions, and internal research are maintained privately.

## Concept summary

A structured catalogue of local brick-and-mortar storefronts, exposed
through an API designed for software agents, starting with a single city
and expanding later. The differentiation explored is agent-native access,
data freshness, and enrichment — not raw store lists, which are widely
available from existing free and commercial sources.

## Validation method

Validation is organized around explicit hypotheses, each with a
predefined signal that would confirm or refute it:

1. **Demand** — do builders of shopping agents struggle to obtain reliable
   local store data today? Tested through problem interviews focused on
   past behavior and current workarounds, not hypothetical interest.
2. **Willingness to pay** — will any segment commit money? Tested with a
   paid pilot offer; a completed payment is treated as stronger evidence
   than verbal enthusiasm.
3. **Free-alternative gaps** — are open and commercial sources genuinely
   insufficient for agent use? Tested by identifying the specific gaps
   interviewees articulate (freshness, schema, licensing, enrichment).
4. **Supply-side interest** — will retailers engage with listing-claim
   and verification offers? Tested with small-batch direct outreach.

Evidence from every conversation is recorded privately and mapped back
to the hypothesis it supports or contradicts.

## Experiments

- **Landing page and waitlist** describing the API with an invented
  example response, used to measure interest signal quality.
- **Free data slice** — one open civic dataset packaged as a clean,
  agent-friendly API, both as a demand probe and as a seed pipeline.
- **Paid sandbox pilot** offered to a small number of interviewees.
- **Retailer outreach** testing the claim-your-listing flow.

## Considerations

- Raw storefront lists are a commodity; the buildable value lies in
  freshness, structure, and agent-shaped access.
- Platform terms of service and data licences govern what may be
  collected, cached, and resold; these are reviewed per source.
- Commercial outreach must comply with applicable anti-spam law.
- Validation benchmarks and kill criteria are set privately before
  experiments begin, so results cannot be rationalized after the fact.

## Monetization options under consideration

- Freemium API tiers for agent developers, up to freshness SLAs.
- Retailer plans for listing claim, verification, and enrichment.
- Bulk or derived-data licensing for enterprise analytics.

Sequencing and pricing decisions are made privately and are not
published here.
